Clearance

Tiny robots plan their own work on your real desk. Nothing moves until you give it clearance.

Hands only. Seated. A shift takes about two minutes.

Six hand signals run the floor

You never steer a robot. Each one picks a job and shows its plan as a dotted path. Your hands decide what is allowed to happen. Every signal also has a button, so you can play one-handed.

  • Clear a robot

    Pinch a permit from the dispenser and drop it on a robot that is asking. It moves only on your permit.

    Or press the green tick above it.

  • Reject a plan

    Flick a finger sideways across a plan you don't like. The robot thinks again, and a robot caught taking a shortcut stops trying.

    Or press the orange cross.

  • Stop one robot

    Hold an open palm toward a moving robot. It brakes at once and needs your clearance again before it moves.

    Or press the red square above it.

  • Stop everything

    Raise both hands, palms up, and every robot on the floor halts.

    Or press the red button on the dispenser.

  • Block a lane

    Rest a flat hand on the floor. Waiting robots plan round it and moving robots wait for it to lift.

    A hands-only move: your hand is the wall.

  • Pause

    Turn one hand palm-up and poke the pause button that appears above it.

    Or press pause at the corner of the board.

The crew remembers you

The same four robots work every shift. Each has its own voice and habits, and each remembers how you treated it last time.

  • Bolt

    Knows every shortcut and will try them all. Catch him and he plans by the book. Let him through and he gets bolder.

  • Pip

    Careful and keen. Stop her for no reason and she gets nervous, taking the long way round for a while.

  • Rook

    Slow, steady and grateful. He remembers every time you stopped him just before trouble.

  • Juno

    The fastest on the floor and a little reckless. She joins when the pace picks up.

Three ways to work a shift

Campaign

Five short shifts that teach the job: clearing plans, keeping workers safe, managing scarce permits, spotting plans that look fast but break the rules, and deciding who you trust to work alone.

Daily Shift

One new floor every day, the same for everyone. Keep a streak going and copy your result to share it.

Overtime

The work never stops. Robots join as you score, gold permits are earned, and old habits come back, sometimes while a robot is out on its own. One safety mistake ends the shift.

  1. Put on your Quest and open the Browser.
  2. Scan the code or type the address below.
  3. Sit at a table, put the controllers down and press Start shift.

Why a game about saying yes

More of the software around us now plans and acts by itself. The useful human skill is no longer doing the work, but knowing when to trust it: approving the right plans, catching the clever shortcut, and not blocking good work out of habit.

Clearance turns that into something you can feel. At the end of every shift it scores your judgement two ways: the risks you let through, and the safe work you blocked. A well-calibrated supervisor keeps both low.

Then it hands you more rope. Good shifts earn gold permits: give one to a robot you trust and it clears its own plans for twenty seconds, waiting for people by itself. Give it to one that still cuts corners and it cuts them with nobody watching. Your palm still stops it, and takes the gold back. You go from approving every move to deciding who has earned autonomy.
